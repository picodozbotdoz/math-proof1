## F1 — Dims 2–4 (18 weights): the quotient is cyclic, `D = I_k`

At all six dim-2 weights `Ik = D = 1` (so `𝕋 = ℤ[T₂]` — the b1 index will
make this an equality theorem).  At all twelve dims-3–4 weights:
`SNF(A) = (1,…,1,D)` — **`𝕋/ℤ[T₂]` is cyclic of order `Ik = D`**, largest
invariant factor = D (`inv_last_eq_D=1`), and `𝕋 = ℤ[T₂,T₃]` (`idx3 = 1`).
This is exactly iteration23-review §6.2's classical prediction (`T₄ = T₂² −
2^{k−1}·T₁` puts `T₁, T₂, T₄` in `ℤ[T₂]`, so the quotient is generated by
`[T₃]`).  Formalizable: generic cyclicity for `n ≤ 4` + the order bridge
(F5) + the landed `D ∣ Ik` certificates ⟹ `D = Ik` with **no new
per-weight certificates**.

## F2 — Dims 5–10 (36 weights): NOT cyclic; `T₃` carries almost all of `I_k`

`cyc=0` everywhere dims ≥ 5.  The single-generator lattices all fail: no
`m ≤ 30` with `𝕋 = ℤ[T₂,T_m]` at ANY dims-5–10 weight (probe 1; greedy
probe 2 never picks past 7, so this persists).  But `T₃` is nearly enough:

* `idx3 := [𝕋 : ℤ[T₂,T₃]]` is tiny relative to `Ik`: 2–46 (dims 5–6),
  20–700 (dim 7), 49–106 580 (dim 8), 5 600–6 580 000 (dim 9),
  4 800–2 100 000 000 (dim 10).  `ℤ[T₂,T₃]/ℤ[T₂]` has order `Ik/idx3`,
  which strictly exceeds `D = Dred` at dims ≥ 5 — **the sub-quotient
  generated by `[T₃]` (order `D`) is a proper subgroup of
  `ℤ[T₂,T₃]/ℤ[T₂]`**; products like `[T₃²] = [T₉ + 2^{k−1}T₁]` genuinely
  add classes.
* Greedy chains (probe 2, primes ≤ 47, add smallest prime lowering the
  index most): **`{3,5}` suffices at 22 weights** (all dims 5–6, plus w88,
  92, 94, 98, 100, 104, 106, 110, 116, 122), **`{3,5,7}` at the remaining
  14** (w84, 90, 96, 102, 108, 112, 114, 118, 120, 124, 126, 128, 130,
  134).  The residual index after `{3,5}` is always a power-product of
  small primes (7, 21, 49, 3) — T₇ finishes it everywhere.

## F3 — The certificate-FREE generic theorem behind F2

`dim S_k ≤ 10` ⟹ `𝕋 = span_ℤ{T_1,…,T_n}` with `n ≤ 10`, and every
`T_j, j ≤ 10` lies in `ℤ[T₂,T₃,T₅,T₇]` **by multiplicativity alone**:
`T₁ = 1`, `T₄ = T₂² − 2^{k−1}T₁`, `T₆ = T₂T₃`, `T₈ = T₂T₄`, `T₉ = T₃² −
2^{k−1}T₁`, `T₁₀ = T₂T₅` (all from `heckeTM_mul`, gcd 1 or the p²
recurrence).  So `𝕋 = ℤ[T₂,T₃,T₅,T₇]` at **all 54 weights with zero
per-weight certificates** — a generic theorem from landed infrastructure.
(The probe explains why nothing past 7 is ever needed: indices stop at
`n ≤ 10`.  At dim ≥ 12, `T₁₁` would enter — out of verified range.)

## F4 — Certificate cost model for the per-weight refinements

* Monomial rows `T₂^a T₃^b T₅^c` (total degree ≤ n) in Miller coordinates:
  entries ≤ 139 d (w60), 294 d (w88), 369 d (w96) — the same class as the
  landed iter-23 `wp` certificates (≤ 341 d).  Entrywise kernel identities
  over such entries are proven-cheap.
* `T₇ = ∑ c_ν · monomial_ν` representations exist (lattice index 1) but the
  **naive HNF-derived coefficient vector exceeds 4 300 digits** (probe 3)
  — do NOT use raw representation certificates.  Prefer either
  (a) a **unimodular-basis certificate**: n monomial-combination rows with
  `|det| = 1` (each row an entrywise identity over ≤ ~400 d entries, det by
  `eval_det`), or (b) the **index form**: an explicit integer basis of
  `ℤ[T₂,T₃]` with `|det| = idx3` (b1's machinery, numbers ≤ 10 d) and of
  `ℤ[T₂,T₃,T₅]` with `|det| = 1`.  The open sub-question "does a plain
  n-subset of monomials already have det ± 1" is a G1 measurement (the
  orchestrator's throwaway greedy was buggy; don't trust it either way).
* `idx3` values are ≤ 10 digits — certificates in the index form are small.

## F5 — The order bridge (for b2)

`D` (the reduced denominator of the landed T₃ witness) = the order of
`[T₃]` in `𝕋/ℤ[T₂]`: `D·T₃ = ∑ N_j T₂^j` (landed) gives `D·[T₃] = 0`;
minimality of D (Bézout-reduced) gives the converse.  Combined with b1's
index and cyclicity at `n ≤ 4`: `D = Ik`.  Formally the needed group fact:
surjective `ZMod D →+ G` (generator) ⟹ `|G| ∣ D` (kernel + Lagrange).
