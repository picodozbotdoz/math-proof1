## 3. What worked

1. **The WORKLOG/resume protocol.**  Every interruption — reboot, agent
    stalls, the handoff to an external AI — was survived by dated WORKLOGs +
    checkpoint commits + persistent temp.  The external session explicitly
    executed our WORKLOG plan.  This is the single most validated process
    asset of the iteration.
2. **Fresh-agent-on-documented-plan.**  Re-spawning (rather than resuming a
    context-drained agent) worked for maeda-t3 flawlessly and was the right
    call for galois-full — the failure there was the plan's content, not the
    handoff mechanism (§6).
3. **Verify-by-re-derivation gates.**  Blueprint R1 ("never trust the
    census") was vindicated: the re-derivation found the Iteration-20 probe
    had missed real certificates (k=110 nm1@137, k=134 transp@7403183), and
    the false k=24 anchor was caught at G1 before any Lean was written.
4. **Kernel-friendly standards as a design force.**  The no-`set_option`
    rule produced the staged `Lv` back-substitution, the Bézout-witness
    Rabin route, and the chunk-literal discipline — each *better* engineering
    than an option raise would have been.
5. **The Modal overlay loop.**  `lean_check.py --file` (fresh clone + overlay,
    ~1 min round trip) carried ~60 verification runs this wave at trivial
    cost; the shared volume cache made re-verification after the reboot free.
6. **Memory right-sizing** (user-prompted): requests 32 GiB → 8 GiB default
    with an OOM escalation ladder; alert threshold 64 → 16 GiB; the first
    16 GiB alert fired, was investigated, and closed (no-cache tree-sum
    artifact) — the policy has teeth again.

## 5. Why we got stuck — anatomy of the G2 wall

The stuck task: *instantiate* the abstract Frobenius machinery in the
concrete setting (Galois group of the charpoly acting on the order
`O = ℤ[x₁..xₙ]`, reduce mod a maximal ideal over ℓ, read the cycle type off
the factor degrees).  Three compounding causes:

1. **A search failure masquerading as a math problem.**  The original
    blueprint (orchestrator's) said "Mathlib has no Dedekind theorem; build
    the golden lemma" — and Mathlib *grep for "Dedekind"* agrees.  But
    Mathlib **has it under an unguessable name**:
    `IsArithFrobAt.exists_of_isInvariant` (`RingTheory/Frobenius.lean`).  The
    internal agent built a correct 258-line self-contained substitute
    (the "golden lemma") before discovering the stock theorem mid-G2 while
    hunting for plumbing.  Net effect: the plan then carried a 300–400-line
    instantiation *estimate* premised on machinery that was about to be
    replaced, and the mental model of "hard, bespoke math ahead" shaped two
    more sessions of over-cautious design work.
2. **Design cargo-culting via the WORKLOG.**  The documented plan inherited
    the blueprint's *resultant-transport* step ("Bézout against
    `Res(q,q′)` for unit differences").  The external session deleted that
    entire step: **separability of the reduction already gives distinct
    roots** — a one-line fact.  Our agents executed the documented design
    instead of re-deriving it; the plan's authority (which the reboot
    protocol had just proven valuable) became a anchor.
3. **Context exhaustion + unverified state.**  By session 3 the galois agent
    had ~125 M tokens of accumulated context and stopped making progress
    (16 tool calls in an hour).  Worse, its last milestone (M3, the maximal
    ideal over ℓ) was **committed-adjacent but never compiled** — the
    external session found ~20 errors in it, most trivial (a missing
    `ZMod.algebra` import surfacing as "Unknown constant").  Our agent's own
    error analysis of M3 treated those as deep problems.

Was the stuck problem already solved in Mathlib or TauCeti?

* **Mathlib: partially — and the part it had was the part we lacked.**
  Existence of a Frobenius element with the right congruence: **yes**
  (`IsArithFrobAt.exists_of_isInvariant`).  Jordan-type Sₙ criteria:
  partially (the swap + 3-cycle cases; *not* Wielandt 13.9 — correctly
  avoided by our (n−1)-cycle preprimitivity lemma, itself new).  The
  cycle-type interpretation (orbits ↔ factor degrees, `cycleType` packaging):
  **no** — that is ours (`frob_cycleType_of_factorization` +
  `cycleType_of_frobenius_roots` + the generic assembly).  So the wall was
  ~40% "already in Mathlib under a hard-to-find name", ~60% genuinely ours.
* **TauCeti: no, and it was irrelevant** — greps show TauCeti has no Maeda /
  Hecke-operator / modular-forms content (its "Modular" hits are lattice
  theory) and no Frobenius-cycle-type content (only `IsDedekindDomain`
  commutative algebra).  No duplication risk, and no help available.

## 6. How the other AI succeeded where we stalled

Honest, multi-factor — not a clean outperformance:

1. **It compiled the inherited WIP first.**  Empirical truth (20 errors,
   mostly import-level) replaced the WORKLOG's 300–400-line estimate before
   any planning.  Our protocol *recorded* state but had begun to *trust*
   recorded state over checked state.
2. **It re-derived instead of executing.**  Dropping the resultant transport,
   indexing roots by `rootSet ℂ` directly (killing the conjugation
   packaging), and covering both certificate types with one orbit lemma —
   each a design-level simplification our plan-and-WORKLOG chain had frozen
   in place.
3. **Fresh context.**  The stall pattern (16 tool calls, re-treading
   analysis) is the classic context-drain signature; the external session
   started clean.
4. **But it started from our accumulated assets**: the pre-verified API-facts
   list in the WORKLOG (it cites them), the committed green certificate
   slice, the G1 certificate data for all 30 weights, the mirror tooling, and
   the reviews/audit infrastructure.  The resolution was *cumulative* — the
   wave's checkpoint discipline is what made the handoff cheap.  The correct
   lesson is not "other AI better" but "handoff-ready beats heroics, and
   fresh-context execution of a documented plan beats exhausted continuation
   of it" — plus "compile the artifact before believing the document".

## 7. Did referring to Mathlib / TauCeti mislead us?

* **Mathlib, wrong ref** (iteration-18b, recurred as a documented class):
  greping a master checkout instead of the pin produced nonexistent API
  names and a failed build round.  This wave the rule ("only
  `.lake/packages/mathlib`") was followed — zero recurrences.
* **Mathlib, right ref, wrong search**: the real cost this iteration was
  *name-shaped search* against a library with unpredictable naming
  (`IsArithFrobAt`).  `exact?`/`apply?` and file-level reading
  (`RingTheory/Frobenius.lean` end-to-end) found in minutes what name-greps
  missed across two sessions.  Recommendation: for any "Mathlib probably
  lacks X" claim that would cost >200 lines to build, spend one
  concept-level search pass (file browsing + `exact?` on the goal shape)
  *before* building.
* **TauCeti**: no mathematical interference (nothing to interfere — §5).
  Its influence was the coding standards, which consistently *helped*
  (§3.4).  One caution stands from earlier iterations: TauCeti's code must
  never be *imported* or greped for API truth (different project, different
  pins) — treated as a standards reference only, which is how this wave used
  it.
* **Blueprint authority as a failure mode** — the orchestrator's own specs
  were the source of both mathematical errors (false T₃ anchor; unnecessary
  resultant step) and of the "Mathlib lacks Dedekind" premise.  The gates
  (G1 re-derivation, compile-first) caught all three.  Keep the gates;
  downgrade blueprints from "plan to execute" to "hypotheses to test".

## 10. Recommendations for Iteration 22

1. Keep the wave structure (blueprints → parallel worktrees → gates →
   union-merge), and keep WORKLOGs — but add the rule the external session
   modeled: **compile the inherited state before planning**.
2. Cap any single agent's lineage at ~2 sessions / ~60 M tokens; then
   re-spawn fresh with a reconstruction briefing (this worked everywhere it
   was tried).
3. Add a **concept-search pass** (file reading + `exact?`) before building
   any >200-line "Mathlib lacks this" component.
4. Treat census/probe data and blueprints as untrusted inputs to gates, never
   as ground truth (both produced errors this wave; both were caught by
   re-derivation).
5. Budget integration explicitly: union-merges of three branches cost ~15
   conflict resolutions — cheap, but only because registration files were
   append-shaped; keep new modules append-shaped in those lists.
