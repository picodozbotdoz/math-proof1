A Galois-group certificate that a proof kernel can check

Two factorizations modulo two primes prove that a polynomial has Galois group Sₙ. This replaced resolvent identities that once needed 202 GiB of kernel memory.

WHAT IS PROVED

bijective_of_frobenius: a monic integer polynomial of degree n ≥ 3, irreducible over ℚ, with one Frobenius of cycle type (n−1) and one that is a transposition, has Galois group Sₙ. frob_cycleType_of_factorization produces those Frobenius elements from a factorization modulo a prime.

Proving that a polynomial has full Galois group is a routine computer algebra query, and it was surprisingly hard to do in a proof checker. The method below is the most reusable thing the Maeda work produced, because it says nothing about modular forms. It applies to any monic integer polynomial.

Why the textbook route failed

Mathlib's standard criterion for prime degree needs exactly two non-real roots. Hecke polynomials have only real roots, so it can never apply. The project therefore built a separate proof per degree, using resolvents. That approach got steadily worse. At degree 5, the sextic resolvent needs identities of degree 24 in the five roots. A monolithic ring attempt ran out of memory at 323 GiB, and even the kernel certificates first needed up to 202 GiB (the memory story). Degree 6 would have been far worse.

The certificate

Dedekind's theorem says a factorization modulo a prime ℓ reveals a Galois element: factor degrees become cycle lengths. So for a degree-n polynomial, it is enough to exhibit:

  • a prime ℓ₁ where it factors as (linear) × (irreducible of degree n − 1), which gives an (n−1)-cycle;
  • a prime ℓ₂ where it factors as n − 2 distinct linear factors × (irreducible quadratic), which gives a transposition.

Irreducibility supplies transitivity. A transitive group with an (n−1)-cycle is primitive, and a primitive group with a transposition is all of Sₙ.

A worked example: weight 72

The characteristic polynomial of T₂ on S₇₂ has degree 6 and starts X⁶ − 66157336440 X⁵ − …. Its certificate is:

  • ℓ₁ = 73: (X − 19)(X⁵ + 38X⁴ + 68X³ + 32X² + 49X + 23), type [5, 1];
  • ℓ₂ = 23: (X − 5)(X − 12)(X − 18)(X − 19)(X² + 4X + 7), type [2, 1, 1, 1, 1].

Both products were multiplied back out for this article and match the polynomial modulo 73 and 23. In Lean, the factorization is a kernel-decided list identity, and the irreducible factor gets a Rabin certificate.

Three things that were not obvious

Mathlib already had the hard part, under another name

Searching Mathlib for "Dedekind" finds nothing useful. The Frobenius existence theorem is there as IsArithFrobAt.exists_of_isInvariant, in RingTheory/Frobenius.lean. An agent wrote a correct 258-line substitute before anyone noticed. The genuinely missing part was the reading of cycle type from factor degrees, which is cycleType_of_frobenius_roots.

The (n−1)-cycle is doing real work

The first plan used a cheaper-looking rule: transitive + transposition + a p-cycle with p ≤ n − 3 gives Sₙ. That is false. The wreath product S₃ ≀ S₂ ⊂ S₆ is transitive and has a transposition and a 3-cycle, yet its order is only 72. Mathlib's correct Jordan theorem for prime cycles is still a to-do there. So JordanPrimitive.lean proves the elementary lemma that a transitive group with an (n−1)-cycle is primitive, by a direct block argument.

Transpositions are rare, and that sets the size of the primes

By Chebotarev, Frobenius elements follow the group's distribution. An (n−1)-cycle has density 1/(n−1), so small ℓ suffice (at most 199 for the T₂ data). A transposition has density 1/(2(n−2)!), about 1 in 80,640 at n = 10. That is why a few weights fell through to a random scan and ended up with ℓ around 7 to 8 million. At that size the kernel's recursion for Fermat inverses overflowed near ℓ ≈ 5·10⁶, so the certificates supply Bézout witnesses instead. The kernel multiplies and compares, and never inverts anything.

SCOPE

The method proves Gal = Sₙ, not smaller groups, and needs suitable primes to exist, which Chebotarev guarantees when the group is Sₙ. Certificates are found by outside search, and the kernel only checks them, so a bad certificate fails and can never pass. The final instantiation of this work was completed by an external AI session after internal agents stalled. The project's review checked that work against logs, statements and audits.
