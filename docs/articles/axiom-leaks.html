116 of 116: how Classical.choice leaks into decide

The first project-wide audit found that every computational certificate broke the project's own axiom rule. Here are the causes, the fixes and a recipe for other Lean projects.

WHAT WAS FOUND

The project required computational theorems to depend on propext only. The first project-wide audit found 116 decide certificates, and all 116 depended on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Today the same audit covers 1,312 certificates and none goes beyond propext.

Nothing was wrong with those proofs, and the three axioms are standard in Mathlib. The point of the rule is different: a statement like "this finite computation returns true" should rest on the computation, not on classical logic. The surprise is that the rule was stated, believed, and false for the entire library, and only a mechanical audit could show that.

One statement, four sets of axioms

A small probe pinned down the cause. Here is the same fact, "this polynomial has no root mod 37", phrased in different ways:

Shape of the decideAxioms
∀ x : ZMod 37, …propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound
Only evaluating a ZMod functionpropext, Quot.sound
∀ x : Fin 37, …propext
∀ y : ℕ, y < 37 → … % 37 ≠ 0, arithmetic in ℤnone

Where they come from

  • Fintype quantifiers. ∀ x : ZMod ℓ is decided through Fintype.decidableForallFintype, which goes through Finset and Multiset and picks up choice.
  • ZMod's ring instance. It uses funext, which needs Quot.sound.
  • Finset.sum, Finset.range and ArithmeticFunction.sigma inside a computed definition.
  • Innocent operators. != on ℤ routes through Int.instLinearOrderPackage.
  • Matrices (found later, in Iteration 22). decide on a matrix equality uses Fintype.decidablePiFintype, whose proof part carries a classical Iff.

The recipe

  1. State certificates over ℕ and ℤ, with bounded quantifiers. Prove once, by hand, that this implies the ZMod statement. The project's HornerCert module does this with a structural Horner evaluation.
  2. Compute with List, not Finset. Then prove once that the list version equals the Mathlib one. MaedaCertC does this for all Maeda coefficient tables.
  3. Decide matrices entrywise: ext a b; fin_cases a <;> fin_cases b <;> decide +kernel.
  4. Audit mechanically, on every release.

The classical axioms can stay in the bridge lemma. The aim is for the computation itself to be clean.

theorem scaled_id : (∑ j ∈ Finset.range 2, cert j • M2 ^ j)
    = (1 : ℤ) • M := by
  ext a b
  fin_cases a <;> fin_cases b <;> decide +kernel

The audit tool

MathProof/AxCheckDecide.lean adds two commands. #audit_decide_certs finds every decide certificate in the light library and reports those depending on more than propext. #axiom_path ax n prints the chain of constants that pulls axiom ax into n, which turns a mystery into a fix.

Two related kernel facts

  • Nat.Prime is not kernel-decidable, because minFac uses well-founded recursion. A plan with .Prime filters inside a decide could not work as written. LehmerCoverageCount uses a structural trial-division check, noFactorFrom, with one correctness lemma.
  • Structural recursion matters generally. A definition that compiles to well-founded recursion stops the kernel from reducing it, often with no warning at the definition.
SCOPE

The audit counts decide certificates in the light library. Heavy modules carry their own #print axioms lines, and analytic theorems use the standard three axioms by design. The clean certificates do not make the top-level theorems constructive.

In TauCeti

About this section.

No matching audit found. The goals also differ: TauCeti's CI allows the three standard axioms in every proof, so the propext-only rule on this page is stricter than TauCeti's own, and a decide depending on Classical.choice is fine there.
