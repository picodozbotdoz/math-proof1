What this project actually found

Twenty-two research iterations produced a large Lean library, and the interesting results are buried in it. These ten articles pull them out, each stating plainly what is proved and what is not.

FIRST, THE HONEST SUMMARY

No open conjecture has been solved here. Lehmer's conjecture and Maeda's conjecture remain open, and ordinary computations reach much further than these formal ones: about 8·10²³ for τ(n) ≠ 0 and weight 12,000 for Maeda. Most of the theorems are classical.

What is new is that they are machine-checked, with computations confirmed by Lean's kernel alone. The project also produced new exact counts, reusable methods, and measured facts about how proof kernels behave. Claims of "first formalization" mean we found no earlier one, not that none exists.

Mathematics

New exact count99.9093%: what is actually proved about τ(n) ≠ 0 below 2³²Three proof routes combine into one theorem. Counted exactly, it reaches all but 3,894,333 of the 4,294,967,295 positive integers below 2³², and the gap has a precise shape. Classical theorem, new formal proofA zero of τ can only first appear at a primeLehmer's 1947 reduction removes every exponent from the problem. The formal proof needs no Deligne bound, just a two-way case split on how divisible τ(p) is by p. Classical congruences, new formal proofsFive more Ramanujan congruences, and a sieve that replaces computationWeights 16 to 26 get their own τ-style theory. Their large moduli let a single residue test prove nonvanishing up to 1.7 million with no dense computation at all. Formal verification of an open conjecture's instancesMaeda's conjecture, machine-checked in 54 weightsFor every level-one weight with 2 ≤ dim S_k ≤ 10, both T₂ and T₃ have irreducible characteristic polynomials with full symmetric Galois group, proved from the Hecke operator itself. Reusable toolA Galois-group certificate that a proof kernel can checkTwo factorizations modulo two primes prove that a polynomial has Galois group Sₙ. This replaced resolvent identities that once needed 202 GiB of kernel memory. New witnesses and a new observationT₃ as an explicit polynomial in T₂, and what its denominators measureAll 54 weights now have rational formulas such as T₃ = 195660 − 48·T₂. Their denominators are not noise: up to dimension 4 they equal the index of ℤ[T₂] in the Hecke ring.

Methods and negative results

MethodMultiplying polynomials inside a proof kernelPacking a whole polynomial into one integer took the kernel-verified bound for τ from 512 to 65,536, a 128-fold gain, after list arithmetic had failed above 71 GB. Method27 GiB per million operations: the memory law of kernel computationThe kernel keeps everything until a declaration ends. Once that was measured, the heaviest module dropped from 202 GiB to 13.5 GiB without changing the mathematics. Method116 of 116: how Classical.choice leaks into decideThe first project-wide audit found that every computational certificate broke the project's own axiom rule. Here are the causes, the fixes and a recipe for other Lean projects. Negative resultsWhat the checks caught: a catalogue of confident mistakesFalse anchors, an unsound certificate scheme, a false group-theory lemma and a theorem Mathlib already had. Each was stated with confidence, and each was stopped by a check.

Pointers to TauCeti

TauCeti is a separate, large Lean 4 library built on Mathlib, with extensive modular forms, Hecke and Galois theory. Each article ends with an "In TauCeti" section listing what a researcher on that topic should look at next.

  • Snapshot. Everything refers to commit 0d44ef6b (2 November 2026), and links are pinned to it. TauCeti changes quickly, so check the current tree too.
  • What was checked. We read each statement and confirmed each name in the cited file. Nothing was compiled against this project, and these are statements as written in TauCeti, not results we verified.
  • No dependency. MathProof does not import TauCeti, so no result on this site relies on these pointers. TauCeti's proofs here are independent of this project's. "Not in TauCeti" means searches of that snapshot found nothing, not that a thing is impossible to find there.
  • Labels. Same result: essentially this project's statement. More general: a stronger or wider version. Ingredient: something the argument uses. Related: same topic, different statement.

The main surprise is in Galois certificates: TauCeti has the same criterion. The closest things to a next step are in Hecke denominators and Maeda. TauCeti contains no τ function, Lehmer or Maeda content.

The record: 22 iterations between 23 October and 2 November 2026, 1,312 audited decide certificates, and the full research log. New to the mathematics? Start with the Langlands program, by example.
