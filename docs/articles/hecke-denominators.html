New witnesses and a new observation

T₃ as an explicit polynomial in T₂, and what its denominators measure

All 54 weights now have rational formulas such as T₃ = 195660 − 48·T₂. Their denominators are not noise: up to dimension 4 they equal the index of ℤ[T₂] in the Hecke ring.

WHAT IS PROVED IN LEAN

heckeTP_commute: Hecke operators commute, at level one, for all primes and all weights. heckeT3_eq_aeval_rat_weightKK, 54 theorems: T₃ = Q(T₂) for an explicit Q ∈ ℚ[X], at every weight with 2 ≤ dim Sk ≤ 10. Over ℂ, every T_p is a polynomial in T₂.

Since Iteration 23: hecke_dual (T_m = ∑ a_m(g_j) T_j over the Miller basis, every m), hecke_independent and the Hecke ring heckeRing ⊇ ℤ[T₂]; at all 54 weights the index I_k = |det A| with the certificate ∑ w′_j T₂^j = I_k T₃, and D ∣ I_k for the reduced denominator D (HeckeIndexWeights, HeckeIndexSmallWeights); every T_m is in ℚ[T₂] (heckeTM_rat_weightKK); and at the six dimension-2 weights every T_m is a_m(g₁) + a_m(g₂)·T₂, with explicit integer coefficients at weights 28–38 (HeckeFieldDim2).

WHAT IS NOT IN LEAN

The denominator formula in the second half of this article was found while writing it. It has a short classical proof and has been checked exactly at all 54 weights by a committed script, but only its divisibility half (D ∣ I_k) is formalized: the equality D = I_k up to dimension 4, and the identification of I_k with the lattice index [𝕋 : ℤ[T₂]] as a cardinality, are not. At weight 24 the integer form of the dimension-2 theorem is not stated (its basis data pins only finitely many coefficients). We do not claim it is new to the literature, since the ingredients are standard.

The theorem, and a trap in it

If T₂ has irreducible characteristic polynomial, anything commuting with it should be a polynomial in it. The first attempt to state this failed for a telling reason. Sk is a complex vector space, and over ℂ no characteristic polynomial of degree 2 or more is irreducible. The fix was indirect. Irreducibility over ℚ gives separability, so the roots are distinct and the minimal polynomial equals the characteristic polynomial. That makes T₂ a cyclic operator, and exists_aeval_of_commute_of_minpoly_eq_charpoly finishes.

That proof is not constructive and produces complex coefficients. Iteration 22 did something stronger and simpler for T₃: it wrote the polynomial down. Each weight carries an integer identity

Σj<n Nj · M₂j = D · M₃

decided entry by entry in the kernel. Commutativity is not needed at all here, since the identity is the proof. The largest numbers have 381 digits.

The observation: denominators measure an index

Let 𝕋 be the ring generated over ℤ by all Hecke operators on Sk, and let f₁, …, fₙ be the Miller basis, the integral forms with ai(fj) = δij for i, j ≤ n. Since a₁(Tm g) = am(g), the classical duality between forms and Hecke operators gives

Tm = Σj≤n am(fj) · Tj

So T₁, …, Tₙ is a ℤ-basis of 𝕋. The relation T₂ T2i = T2i+1 + 2k−1 T2i−1 shows the powers of T₂ span the same lattice as T₁, T₂, T₄, …, T2n−1. The index of ℤ[T₂] in 𝕋 is therefore a determinant of q-coefficients:

Ik = [𝕋 : ℤ[T₂]] = | det ( a2i(fj) ) |,   0 ≤ i < n, 1 ≤ j ≤ n

The reduced denominator D of the T₃ witness is the order of T₃ in the finite group 𝕋 / ℤ[T₂], so D divides Ik. The small dimensions follow directly:

  • Dimension 2. 𝕋 has basis 1, T₂, so 𝕋 = ℤ[T₂]. Every T_m is an integer polynomial in T₂, with T₃ = a₃(f₁) + a₃(f₂)·T₂. This explains the table above, and it holds for all T_m, not only T₃.
  • Dimensions 3 and 4. ℤ[T₂] already contains 1, T₂, T₄, so the quotient is cyclic and generated by T₃. Hence D = Ik exactly.
  • Dimension 5 and up. The quotient needs T₃ and T₅, so it need not be cyclic, and D may be a proper divisor.

In dimension 3, there is even a one-line closed form:

T₃ = ( T₂² − 2k−1 − a₄(f₁) − a₄(f₂)·T₂ ) / a₄(f₃)

and a₄(f₃) is the q⁴ coefficient of Δ³E₄aE₆b = q³ + (−72 + 240a − 504b)q⁴ + …. So the mysterious denominators can be read straight off that formula:

The prediction that equality holds exactly through dimension 4 and fails from dimension 5 on holds at all 54 weights. The factors of D also show large powers of 2, 3 and 7 (at weight 134: 2¹⁷⁶ · 3¹⁷ · 7⁵ and more). That is expected: T₂ is highly 2-divisible on level-one forms, which makes ℤ[T₂] a thin sublattice at 2. We have not worked out the exponent pattern.

Why it matters. Ik ≠ 0 is exactly the statement that T₂ generates the Hecke algebra over ℚ. The formula says how far T₂ is from generating it over ℤ, using only finitely many q-coefficients. It also suggested one general theorem worth formalizing: integrality of the witnesses at every dimension-2 weight, instead of checking them one weight at a time. Iteration 23 formalized it, for every m at once (HeckeFieldDim2; integer form at weights 28–38).

In TauCeti

TauCeti pointers for readers who want to go further. Statements were read at a pinned commit. Nothing here depends on them.

  • TauCeti.qExpansion_coeff_one_heckeTCuspLevelOne Ingredient
    a₁(Tₙf) = aₙ(f) for all n. This is the identity the duality in this article is built on, already proved.
  • LinearMap.finite_adjoin_of_injective_pairing Ingredient
    An injective pairing makes the generated algebra finite. It is the abstract shape of the forms-operators duality, and a natural starting point for formalizing the index formula.
  • HeckeCosetModule.mul_comm_of_antiInvolution More general
    Shimura's commutativity criterion for abstract Hecke rings. heckeTP_commute here takes a different route, through q-coefficients. We did not find a stand-alone Commute statement for TauCeti's level-one Tₙ.
  • TauCeti.heckeTCuspAlgebra_finite More general
    The ℤ-algebra generated by all Tₙ on S_k(Γ₁(N)) is a finite ℤ-module. This is the Hecke ring 𝕋 of this article, at every level. No rank or basis is given.
  • TauCeti.heckeTDiamondCuspNewRationalAlgebra_finite Related
    The rational Hecke algebra of the newspace is finite-dimensional.
  • TauCeti.Newform.heckeTDiamondCuspNewAlgebraEquiv Related
    The complex newspace Hecke algebra is a product of copies of ℂ indexed by newforms.
  • TauCeti.ModularForm.integralAtCusps Related
    Forms with integral expansions at every cusp, the lattice of Deligne and Serre. The Miller lattice in this article is its level-one analogue at the single cusp.

Not in TauCeti: The Miller basis, a ℤ-basis of 𝕋, any statement that T₂ generates the Hecke algebra, and any index or denominator formula.
